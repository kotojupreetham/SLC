## Trust/proof strip ideas

Use only factual, approved items.

- GitOps and progressive delivery
- Platform engineering foundations
- Security guardrails in the delivery path
- Observability designed into release operations
- Measurable delivery maturity improvement

Avoid unapproved exact promises such as "Zero downtime", "100% secure", "Guaranteed compliance", or unsourced performance figures.

## Service framing templates

### Release Engineering

> **Best for:** teams with slow release windows, manual approval bottlenecks, or risky production deployments.

> **What we change:** release workflows, progressive rollout patterns, automated checks, and rollback readiness.

> **Outcome:** a more repeatable delivery process with clearer risk controls.

### CI/CD and Pipeline Design

> **Best for:** teams whose pipelines are slow, brittle, difficult to trust, or expensive to maintain.

> **What we change:** build architecture, test orchestration, caching, quality gates, and artifact flow.

> **Outcome:** faster feedback and a pipeline that is easier to operate and evolve.

### Platform Engineering

> **Best for:** organisations that want developers to ship safely without every team rebuilding infrastructure patterns.

> **What we change:** paved paths, self-service workflows, policy guardrails, and shared platform capabilities.

> **Outcome:** more consistent delivery with less operational friction for product teams.

### Infrastructure Automation

> **Best for:** teams managing manual infrastructure changes, drift, inconsistent environments, or slow provisioning.

> **What we change:** infrastructure definitions, provisioning workflows, drift detection, and repeatable environment patterns.

> **Outcome:** infrastructure that is easier to reproduce, review, and operate.

### DevSecOps and Compliance

> **Best for:** teams that need security controls embedded in delivery rather than added as a late-stage gate.

> **What we change:** code and dependency scanning, secret handling, artifact verification, and policy checks.

> **Outcome:** faster, more visible risk decisions with fewer manual security handoffs.

### Observability and Incident Engineering

> **Best for:** teams that can deploy but cannot quickly understand release health or recover from failure.

> **What we change:** metrics, logs, traces, alerts, dashboards, and release-to-runtime feedback loops.

> **Outcome:** clearer operational signals and faster learning from production behaviour.

## Pipeline stage content template

Use the same structure for every stage:

```text
Stage name
Plain-language outcome
Input: what must be ready
Control: what automation/policy is applied
Result: what is produced or proven
Example signals: two metrics/status checks
```

Example:

> **Build - create a repeatable artifact**
> Teams need a versioned, traceable artifact that behaves consistently across environments.
> **Input:** reviewed source code and dependency definitions.
> **Control:** deterministic build configuration, caching, security scans, and artifact signing where appropriate.
> **Result:** a versioned deployable artifact with clear provenance.

## Case study title formula

- "Reducing release-window risk for a multi-service financial platform"
- "Making security checks part of the delivery path for a regulated health product"
- "Creating a self-service delivery foundation for a growing product organisation"

## Language to avoid unless contractually/evidentially supported

- guaranteed
- zero downtime
- fully secure
- real-time (for static/demo content)
- secure transmission (without implementation)
- 24-hour response (without operating commitment)
- 100% compliance
- zero vulnerabilities
- instant rollback

Replace with scoped, honest language: "designed to", "helps teams", "supports", "automates", "can reduce", "example", or an approved measured result.
