# 12 - Client Discovery and Approval Questions

## How to use

Use this document in a 45-60 minute client working session before implementing significant changes. These questions avoid inventing facts, legal positions, marketing claims, or integrations.

## A. Business and audience

1. Who is the primary visitor: CTO, VP Engineering, platform leader, release manager, founder, procurement lead, or another role?
2. What is the first problem they should recognise on the homepage?
3. What is the desired conversion: contact form, calendar booking, email, downloadable assessment, or a combination?
4. Is the client selling a discrete service package, retained consulting, a technical audit, or a broader transformation engagement?
5. What sales objection appears most often: cost, trust, scope, security, timeline, or proof of capability?

## B. Positioning and proof

1. Which three services create the most revenue or strategic value?
2. Which technologies are actual strengths versus merely familiar market terms?
3. Which case studies can be named publicly? Which must be anonymous?
4. For every metric: what is the source, period, baseline, definition, and approval owner?
5. Which certifications, partnerships, cloud competencies, or compliance claims can be verified?
6. Is the "control room" demo content, a real system, or a conceptual representation?
7. Can the site use client logos, quotes, headshots, or architecture diagrams? Who approves each?

## C. Form and operations

1. Where should a lead go: CRM, email inbox, shared ticket system, database?
2. Who owns response and what is the truthful target response time?
3. Does the team operate weekends/holidays? If not, do not show an absolute 24-hour promise.
4. What data must be collected at first contact, and what should wait for a discovery call?
5. What spam protection is acceptable to the target audience?
6. Who receives an alert when the form or provider fails?
7. Is a calendar booking tool already in use? If so, which one and what privacy/scripts does it require?

## D. Legal, privacy, and regions

1. What legal entity owns the site and what contact details must appear?
2. Which visitor regions are targeted?
3. Which privacy policy/terms are approved today?
4. What analytics, pixels, recording, chat, or error monitoring tools are planned?
5. Which data processors/subprocessors will receive lead information?
6. How long should leads be retained, and how will deletion/access requests be handled?
7. Does client legal require cookie consent before any nonessential analytics?

## E. Brand and visual direction

1. Is the current dark control-room visual system aligned with the brand, or should it become more conservative/executive?
2. Which competitor sites feel too generic, too playful, or closest to the desired tone?
3. Does the client have an existing logo pack, icon style, typography, photography, diagram style, or brand guidelines?
4. What visual level is acceptable on mobile/low-end devices: feature parity, simplified experience, or static alternate view?
5. Are background effects/particles desirable because they communicate something, or just because they look high-tech?
6. Are illustrations/diagrams or real engineering artifacts preferred over stock photography?

## F. Deployment and operations

1. What is the final production domain and hosting platform?
2. Who owns DNS, deployment access, and rollback decisions?
3. Is staging required? How are previews approved?
4. What browser/device support is expected?
5. Who approves copy, visual design, code, legal content, and production release?
6. What monitoring/analytics budget/tools are acceptable?

## Red flags to escalate, not guess

- Client wants to publish a metric but cannot explain its source.
- Client asks to claim a certification/compliance standard it does not hold.
- Client wants "secure" language without an approved data-handling design.
- Client wants a contact form but has no recipient/response owner.
- Client wants live production data on a public website without data-classification review.
- Client wants user tracking/recording without privacy/legal direction.
- Client asks for a feature that requires paid vendor accounts or external approvals not yet provided.
